# ML02 - Repeated-seed forecasting protocol

## Question

Do the original causal variance-forecast rankings and frozen interval coverage survive independently generated histories, and how much do the errors and coverage vary?

## Method

The ensemble fixes process seeds 100-119 and excludes the original illustrative seed 73. Each generates 2,000 synthetic Gaussian GARCH-like returns. Stable and stress runs share innovations within each seed; different seeds generate independent histories. This gives 20 independent pairs, not 40 independent replicates. No seed is discarded, including explosive stress paths.

The existing six causal close-of-day features predict the next squared return. Each run retains the original partitions: 979 training targets (<1000), 199 validation targets (1001-1199), 249 calibration targets (1201-1449), and 549 test targets (1451-1999). Targets at boundaries 1000, 1200 and 1450 are embargoed between these initial partitions. The stable process uses (omega, alpha, beta)=(0.025, 0.10, 0.84); the stress process switches at day 1450 to (0.12, 0.16, 0.84). Unit persistence in stress deliberately precludes a finite stationary unconditional variance.

The comparison preserves training mean, lagged square, frozen ridge, the 6->12->1 tanh MLP, and expanding ridge. Training-only scaling and fitting are unchanged. Validation MSE selects ridge alpha from [0.01, 1, 10, 100], with the first minimum breaking ties. The grid, objective and model settings are fixed across seeds; alpha is selected separately within each dataset and then frozen, including for expanding refits. There is no selection on calibration/test scores or across ensemble results. The MLP retains initialization seed 53 and 350 full-batch epochs, isolating process variability rather than neural initialization variability.

Frozen ridge bands use the 225th ordered absolute residual among 249 calibration targets, nominal coverage 90%, and lower endpoint clipped at zero. Actual mean width is recorded. Expanding ridge refits every 60 test targets, including all labels strictly before that block. It may learn from earlier test observations, as a causal online forecaster; its intervals are not evaluated. QLIKE remains mean(log(max(prediction, 1e-5)) + target/max(prediction, 1e-5)); floor-hit fractions are retained per model/seed. This loss omits target-only terms and can be negative.

Every seed receives equal weight. The artifacts contain per-seed scores, selected alphas and validation losses, interval radius, width and coverage; summaries include mean, sample SD (ddof=1), median, empirical linear p10/p90, range, and Monte Carlo standard error of the mean (SD/sqrt(20)). Error differences are paired within seed; negative differences favor the first model. Quantiles describe variability across synthetic datasets, not confidence intervals or predictive bands. Dependent target days are never counted as independent replicates.

The paired stress-minus-stable coverage change averages **-70.38 percentage points**, seed SD 6.54 pp and MCSE 1.46 pp. Every pair loses coverage; mean nominal coverage alone would conceal the stable-case spread and severe stress failures.

In stable histories, ridge beats training mean on RMSE in 19/20 seeds (mean paired difference -0.035726, seed SD 0.069318). The MLP beats training mean on MAE in 20/20 seeds. Expanding ridge beats frozen ridge on RMSE in 13/20 seeds, yet its mean RMSE difference is +0.005398 (SD 0.029807): a majority win count does not imply a lower average loss.

In stress histories, expanding ridge beats frozen ridge on RMSE in 16/20 seeds (mean difference -44.972176, SD 194.580849), but worsens MAE in all 20 (mean difference +33.520584, SD 141.713823). Adaptation is therefore not a uniform improvement. Frozen ridge beats training mean on RMSE in 20/20 seeds. Its QLIKE is lower in 18/20 seeds, yet mean QLIKE is much worse: 9,543.59 versus 848.88. Rare floor-sensitive failures dominate the average. These negative findings are retained.

The stress distribution is highly skewed: ridge RMSE mean 783.09, SD 3,335.56, but p10-p90 only 9.37-90.44. The largest ridge stress RMSE is 14,953.16 at seed 115 (next largest 193.43 at seed 108). The full range and per-seed data in the artifacts must accompany interpretation of that mean; p10/p90 alone can conceal extreme tails. Selected alpha counts in both regimes are {0.01: 3, 1: 1, 10: 2, 100: 14}. Identical choices and calibration radii within each pair follow from identical pre-break data, rather than cross-regime tuning.

## Limitations and references

This evaluates one fixed synthetic generator, one shift mechanism, fixed data length and fixed model families. It does not demonstrate market accuracy or performance on licensed public data. Twenty process seeds provide limited tail resolution, especially for the nonstationary stress; MCSE is an estimated simulation precision diagnostic, not a normal-theory significance statement. No architecture, objective, feature ablation or neural-seed comparison was performed. Reusing these holdouts to develop future models would require a fresh confirmation ensemble.

Ordinary split-conformal guarantees rely on exchangeability, which this serially dependent, shifted setup does not provide. See [Angelopoulos and Bates](https://arxiv.org/abs/2107.07511) and [Barber et al., Conformal prediction beyond exchangeability](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.13415). We do not implement their extensions. Dataset-level paired comparisons are informed by [Demsar (2006)](https://www.jmlr.org/papers/volume7/demsar06a/demsar06a.pdf); this work uses descriptive paired summaries, not that paper's classification significance tests.

## What the author should understand for an interview

Explain why new process seeds change the dataset while changing only a neural seed changes the optimizer realization. Identify the independent unit (one history), and why paired stable/stress outcomes and correlated target days cannot be pooled as independent samples. Distinguish sample SD (dataset variability), MCSE (estimated precision of the ensemble mean), empirical quantiles, and predictive interval coverage.

Be able to trace each data boundary: training learns scales and coefficients, validation selects alpha, calibration sets the residual threshold, and holdout evaluates the frozen procedure. Expanding fits legitimately use earlier observed test labels but never future labels. Explain why fixing the selection rule differs from forcing one alpha across all datasets. Finally, explain how a model can win on most seeds but lose on average, how MAE and RMSE reward different behavior, and how tiny variance predictions and explosive synthetic paths can dominate QLIKE. A repeated failure is evidence about this simulation, not a universal theorem about adaptation.
